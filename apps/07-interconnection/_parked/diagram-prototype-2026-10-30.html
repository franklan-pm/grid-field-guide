Interconnection Queue Field Guide

To plug into the transmission grid, a power plant or a data center has to join a queue. The generator queue is long, public and mostly speculative: about 13% of the capacity that enters gets built, and the median project takes 5 years. The load queue is newer and mostly undocumented. In Texas, the one place it can be seen, it is several times the size of the grid it wants to join.

Two queues into one substation

A VPP squeezes more out of the wires we have. But what happens when someone needs a new connection to those wires? A solar-plus-battery plant and a data center can land on the same substation. Each has to ask permission first, in its own queue, and the grid operator studies what equipment would have to be added.

    How to read this

    The drawing

    • Solid lines are electricity. Ink is transmission voltage, orange is medium voltage (the plant's collector lines and the data center's site feeders), and the double blue line is the DC side of the inverter.
    • Dashed boxes are network upgrades: equipment the studies add so the grid can take the new connection. "Run the study" lights the upgrades each project would likely trigger. Which upgrades each triggers is illustrative; real studies decide that project by project.
    • Each project has its own point of interconnection (POI). Here both sit at the same substation. The data center, at 300 MW, is served at transmission voltage and steps the power down in its own customer substation.

    The generator queue

    • The grey bars count projects, not gigawatts, and cover only the 47% of withdrawals whose study phase is recorded. About 11% of those were in a study the data doesn't name, so the five bars add up to 89%.
    • The 13 / 75 / 11 split is by capacity, for requests made in 2000–2020, with status at the end of 2025. Recent requests haven't resolved yet, which is why "still waiting" matters.
    • The two timing brackets are separate medians, and they do not add up to the 61-month median for the whole trip: they come from different samples. Only built projects have a time-to-build.

    The load queue

    • The 6.6 GW that cleared a stability assessment went through the older, case-by-case process. Batch Zero, the new batch study for loads of 75 MW and up, has not run: it was paused on Aug 3, 2026 for an audit due Dec 10, with no new study date.
    • We have no data on where the rest of the load requests left, so there are no exit bars. The bars show how much is left in play.
    • Rules are being written now. FERC reportedly sent show-cause orders to six grid operators on June 18, 2026. That is not a final rule, and ERCOT isn't under FERC's interconnection rules. ⚠ Not yet checked against FERC's documents.

    ⚠ marks a number that is still waiting for a primary-source check. Every figure carries an "as of" date.
