Virtual Power Plant Field Guide

A gas peaker is one big machine in one place, often 50–100 MW. A virtual power plant is thousands of small machines in thousands of homes: thermostats, water heaters, EV chargers, batteries and solar. Software makes them act as one.

What is a virtual power plant?

A virtual power plant (VPP) is thousands of small devices in people's homes, such as thermostats, water heaters, EV chargers and batteries, run by software so they act like one power plant. When the grid is short of power, they all use a little less, or send a little back, at the same time.

For example, Google announced a deal with Voltus to set up a VPP in the PJM grid. Announced on Oct 22, 2026, the deal adds ~1,070 MW of electric capacity to an earlier 100 MW agreement. That's about the size of a large nuclear reactor, but available only when the grid calls for it!

1

Real hardware, in homes

Every device already exists for another reason: to heat the house, charge the car, back up the lights.

2

Many makers' clouds

Each brand runs its own cloud service: ecobee, Google Nest, ChargePoint, Tesla, Enphase…

3

One aggregator on top

VPP software talks to all those clouds at once and adds the devices up into megawatts.

Watch one event

  1. 1The grid asksThe grid operator needs power from 4 to 8 pm, the same way it would call a power plant.
  2. 2The VPP splits the jobThe aggregator works out how much each kind of device can give, then sends one request to each maker's cloud.
  3. 3Each cloud tells its devicesThermostats shift 2–4°F, EV chargers pause, batteries discharge, water heaters wait. Customers can always override.
  4. 4The grid sees one power plantThe power doesn't travel over the internet. It shows up on the wires the homes already use. The aggregator reports the total.
Common mix-up: ecobee, Nest and Tesla aren't the VPP. They run device clouds. The VPP software (Uplight, EnergyHub, Everyday Electric…) sits on top of them, and the utility's DERMS (chapter 4) checks the wires.

How the power numbers are worked out

Each device cuts a little power during the event. Add them all up and you get the fleet's megawatts. Basics and the Playground use the same model. Here it runs for a 95°F summer evening, 4 to 8 pm. (1 kW is a few toasters. 1,000 kW is 1 MW.)

DeviceDevices per 100 homesPower cut per devicePower from 100 homes

  • Thermostat: ecobee's 2025 season, 108 MW ÷ 143,000 thermostats, at 95°F. It fades as homes warm up.
  • Water heater: a typical value, averaged over 4 hours. The tank holds heat, so it can wait.
  • EV charger: a typical value: 7 kW × 60% plugged in × 12% charging at that hour.
  • Home battery: California, Sep 2026, 580 MW ÷ 140,000 batteries. It holds about 3 hours (12 kWh usable).
  • Rooftop solar: no help at 4–8 pm. It filled the batteries at midday.

The mix per 100 homes is a typical one, for illustration, and homes can have several devices. Values marked "typical" are round figures for learning, not engineering numbers.

Cost: DOE projection for a 400 MW VPP, via SEIA. Brattle (2023) put VPP net cost at about 40–60% of the alternatives. In PJM's 2027–28 auction, demand-side resources were credited at 92% of their MW, up from 69%.

What a customer earns

PSE Flex Batteries: $75 per kWh to sign up (up to $1,000), plus $0.50 per kWh discharged in events (up to $500 a year).

Other programs: PSE Flex Smart pays $50 to sign up plus $20 a season per thermostat. California's DSGS paid Powerwall owners $7–$15 per kW-month in 2026, up to about $280 a year (Tesla).

What's at risk

California's Demand Side Grid Support (DSGS) program paid for the 2025 and 2026 records; the separate Emergency Load Reduction Program (ELRP) joined it for the October one. A VPP depends on a program that pays for it.

  • 2022DSGS starts (California Energy Commission).
  • 2023Home batteries can join. About 700 MW enrolled by 2025.
  • Jul 29, 2025535 MW from 100,000+ batteries, held flat 7–9 pm.
  • Sep 9, 2026580 MW from 140,000+ batteries for 3 hours.
  • Oct 6, 2026745 MW, the current record (company-reported). Called by both DSGS and ELRP.
  • Dec 2026Funding runs out. The final 2026–27 budget added no money for 2027, and a plan to move the program to the CPUC was left out. No successor is funded yet.
